## Decisions made (and why)
- Over-masking is the safe side for privacy, but a masked word is lost to the officer, so ordinary words were taken
  from the data by eye, not guessed.
- The "10 Mr" (metre) over-mask seen in an R06 case occurs once in 57,508 descriptions: left alone.
- Committed results are re-masked in place (text-level replacement), not regenerated: their measured numbers stay.

## Problems, traps, surprises
- The masker's tests passed while thousands of names leaked: tests held the patterns someone had thought of. Measure a
  privacy rule on the real descriptions, with a sample of what it leaves, not only what it catches.
- A rule for one place leaks into another: "kothi" and "haveli" are home words in Hindi and also parts of place names
  (Tq. Haveli). Read what a rule masks before keeping it.
- Masking one thing can uncover another: a phone number glued to a name ending in "V" (the number rule skips digits
  after a V, for vendor codes) appeared only once the name was masked.
