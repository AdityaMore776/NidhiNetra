**What the real descriptions say** (dashboard, 40,935 works):
- **Lanes run house to house.** 39% of roads and 45% of paths name houses ("from X's house to Y's
  house").
- **Lanes are short.** The median of the 1,364 lengths stated is 121 m; 40% are 100 m or less.
- **Roads are filed in same-day programmes**, often 2 to 8 lanes by one MP in a day.
- **Hand pumps sit at houses.** A third of borewells are hand pumps "near X's house", at about
  Rs 70,000, filed in batches.

**`data/trial/real_geo_roads.py`** (new) lays each lane between two real houses of Jaunpur's
village cores (Open Buildings), at a real stated length. It tries two layouts: random programmes
of lanes, and the next street over at 15/25/35/50 m a year later. Repeats are filed 6–18 months
later: re-pinned, 30–60 m longer, or its inner 70%. Hand pumps reuse the light programmes' real
days and sizes.

**My first parallel-streets run was wrong.** I left the first lane of each pair out of the index,
so those rows compared nothing. Fixed, and the run then showed the real problem:
- the next street over was asked 93% (15 m apart), 81% (25 m) and 64% (35 m) of the time;
- almost all by "both ends within 50 m", because real lanes are short.

**Rules tried** (each in the results):
- ends at 30 m;
- the line at 15 or 20 m;
- "ends AND on its line" at several distances;
- for the "longer / part of it" case, the line at 20 or 25 m.

**Switched:**
- **Roads:** both ends within 30 m AND 80% of it within 20 m of the other's line; otherwise its
  line within 25 m, for a road filed longer or as a part. The next street over is now asked 28%
  (25 m apart), 3% (35 m) and 1% (50 m) of the time; random lanes 10% (was 12%). Repeats caught:
  99% re-pinned, 94% longer, 98% part (was 100, 97, 100).
- **Hand pumps:** `borewell` joins `POINT_FAMILIES` and `SAME_DAY_APART`. Honest pumps asked fall
  from 86% to 1%; 98 of 100 repeats are still asked.

## Decisions made (and why)
- **"Ends AND line", not tighter ends alone.**
  - Ends at 30 m alone still asked 41% of lanes on the next street 25 m away.
  - The line at 15 m lost 10–16% of repeats.
  - Requiring both is what separates a lane filed again (both ends near and on the same line)
    from the next street (ends near, line apart).
- **At 15 m apart, no rule separates two streets.** Phone GPS wanders about that much. 76% are
  still asked, and the first site video settles it.
- **Same-day programmes of roads are still compared**, unlike lights and pumps. A lane filed twice
  on one day is one lane, and a road's two ends and line make the match much more certain than one
  pin.
