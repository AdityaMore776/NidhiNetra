## What I did
T139 fitted Duplicate Work's one-site rules (lights and pumps as one spot, programmes apart,
purchases by kind, roads by ends AND line) to Jaunpur only. This checks them in districts whose
villages look different. Each district uses its own real works, real post offices (`indiapins`)
and real buildings (Open Buildings, about 25-45 s to fetch per district, kept in the git-ignored
`data/raw/open_buildings/`).

- **Districts:** Kheri (Terai), Bokaro (plateau hamlets), Puri (coast), Howrah (dense,
  peri-urban), Palakkad (dispersed homesteads) and Kheda (compact villages, roads only).
- **`real_geo_check.configure(district)`:**
  - takes the district's post offices, dropping positions 0.6 degrees or more from their median
    (typos);
  - builds the box and Open Buildings cells around them, and its own building file and district
    code;
  - gives the district its own flat-map origin. Jaunpur's was used everywhere before, which would
    be 8% off in metres in Kerala. Jaunpur's results are unchanged (re-run, no diff).
- **`real_geo_districts.py`** (new) runs lights (10 a village, in a hamlet or anywhere), hand
  pumps, random lane programmes and the next street over at 25 and 35 m. Each runs under the rules
  before T139 and now.

## Decisions made (and why)
- **No rule changes.** Every district behaves like Jaunpur: houses stand 7.7–11.2 m apart, and
  the same rows fall by the same amounts.
- **The 20 m light line catches 86–94% of repeats** when lights are spread through a village.
  That is the honest measure of it; a hamlet's neighbours inflate the other row. The misses are
  the phone fixes that wander 15 m or more. Widening the line would bring the neighbours back.
