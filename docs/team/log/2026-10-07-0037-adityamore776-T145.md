## What I did
The owner: the cost "size prediction" or "price prediction" "should give a range and not just
'should be quantity 1334'".
- **Engine:**
  - `cost_form/norms.py`: each need has a `low`, the standard amount for the size (the mix, the
    area, the count, the pipe's length with 6 m a house connection). Its `needed` is the same
    figure with the room honest estimates carry.
  - `rates.py`: a fair rate carries the lowest and highest of the other agencies' own median
    rates. A state-wide rate has its leeway on both ends; a schedule rate is its own range.
  - `whole.py`: the whole estimate's fair price at those lowest and highest rates. Each line's
    "others here" range and "the size takes" range appear in its sentence.
  - `labour.py`: what other agencies pay a trade, lowest to highest.
  - `agent.py`, `validate.py`: the findings, the notes and the form's confirm text say ranges.
    The per-unit price gives the middle 8 in 10 of similar works.
  - `explain.py`: passes the new facts on.
- **Website:**
  - `lib/work-form-flags.ts` writes every flag and field label as a range;
  - the end-to-end page shows ranges per line, for labour and for the whole estimate;
  - the explain page shows the fair-price range.
- **Examples rebuilt** (`work_form_examples.py`, `end_to_end_examples.py`, `showcase_examples.py`).

## Decisions made (and why)
- **The range describes; it does not decide.** What is asked is still the upper end against the
  owner's margins, so no flag changed.
- **A rate's range is the other agencies' own medians, lowest to highest**: the same forms the
  fair rate (their median) comes from.
- **One figure when both ends meet** (a count of lights, a schedule rate).
